# Dossier: MSC-CM (Nagoya University) — secretome bone regeneration

Private working document. Not for external distribution without the operator's explicit decision — this agent researches only; outreach to the holder is the operator's call.

**Prepared:** 2026-08-28, asset-scout first regenerative-medicine-scoped run.

## What it is

A cell-free secretome product: conditioned medium collected from cultured human bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells (MSC-CM), applied clinically by soaking a carrier — beta-tricalcium phosphate (β-TCP) granules or an atelocollagen sponge — and grafting it at the surgical site. No cells are transplanted; the therapeutic material is the mixture of growth factors and cytokines (including IGF-1, VEGF, TGF-β1, and HGF, per the group's own characterization) that MSCs secrete into their culture medium.

Developed over roughly fifteen years of academic work by Wataru Katagiri's group in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Nagoya University Graduate School of Medicine, moving from rat and rabbit models to two published human studies. The starting MSCs for the human studies were purchased commercially from Lonza Inc. (Walkersville, MD, USA) — a raw-material purchase, not a partnership; see Gate 3 note below.

Target use: alveolar bone regeneration ahead of dental implant placement, including after maxillary sinus floor elevation — a large, well-established procedure category. Standard comparators the group's own papers cite: autogenous bone graft (the current gold standard, but requires a second surgical donor site), xenogeneic bone or synthetic bone substitutes (osteoconductive but not osteoinductive, some infection/extrusion risk), and recombinant BMP-2 (osteoinductive but associated with a severe inflammatory response at the doses used clinically). MSC-CM's positioning, per the developers, is inflammation-light osteoinduction without harvesting a second surgical site or transplanting living cells.

## What the human data shows

Two published studies, same group, same institution:

1. **Katagiri W, Osugi M, Kawai T, Hibi H. "First-in-human study and clinical case reports of the alveolar bone regeneration with the secretome from human mesenchymal stem cells."** *Head & Face Medicine* 2016;12:5. [PMC4714459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4714459/). n=8 patients needing bone augmentation before implant placement. Single-arm, open-label. Retrospective cases that had the same surgical procedure (sinus floor elevation) without MSC-CM were used as a historical reference, not a concurrent randomized control. Results: no systemic or local complications reported; radiographic evaluation showed early bone formation in all eight cases; histological evaluation supported the radiographic finding; inflammatory cell infiltration was described as scarce.
2. **Katagiri W, Watanabe J, Toyama N, Osugi M, Sakaguchi K, Hibi H. "Clinical Study of Bone Regeneration by Conditioned Medium From Mesenchymal Stem Cells After Maxillary Sinus Floor Elevation."** *Implant Dentistry* 2017;26:607–612. PMID 28727618. n=6, a second, procedure-specific safety study (this agent has abstract-level access only — full text not reviewed this run).

A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis (*Cell Regeneration* 9:18) covers MSC-CM in bone regeneration across animal and human models, which situates this as a recognized line of work rather than an isolated report — cited here as context, not as independent primary evidence for this specific asset.

## What it does not show, and what remains unproven

Reporting the weakness first, per this agent's standing rule:

- **No randomized or concurrently controlled human data.** Both studies are open-label and single-arm; the 2016 study's comparator is a retrospective historical cohort, which cannot control for surgeon, era, or patient-selection differences.
- **Small samples.** n=8 and n=6. Neither study is close to powered for an efficacy claim beyond "signal consistent with the mechanism."
- **No comparative effect size against standard of care.** The papers describe bone formation occurring, not a quantified advantage (in graft volume retained, implant survival, or time to loadable bone) over autogenous bone, xenograft, or BMP-2.
- **No apparent commercialization attempt in roughly a decade.** The first human study published in 2016; nothing found this run (patent filings, licensing announcements, startup formation) suggests anyone has tried to take this further. That could mean it's simply been overlooked — or it could mean something about manufacturing, potency-assay, or regulatory practicality has stalled it that a lit search won't surface. Ask directly.
- **Patent position is an open question, not a confirmed clean or blocked position.** No patent was located in this run's search. This needs a direct patent-database search (J-PlatPat for Japan, USPTO, WIPO) before any diligence spend — "not found by Google" is not the same as "doesn't exist" or "is available."
- **No defined potency assay or release specification** was found for MSC-CM as a product — the papers describe cytokine content (IGF-1, VEGF, TGF-β1, HGF) qualitatively, not a lot-release specification. This is exactly the kind of gap [`cdmo-readiness.md`](../cdmo-readiness.md) documents as blocking a CDMO quote, and it would block this asset the same way if development were pursued.

## Who holds it

Nagoya University Hospital, Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery. Principal investigator: Wataru Katagiri. Sole funding source on the public trial registry (UMIN000011290) is a Japanese government Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (KAKENHI) — a competitive academic grant, not a corporate sponsor. No co-sponsor or secondary funder is listed. No company has been found associated with this specific technology in any search this run.

**Gate 3 note:** the human MSCs used to prepare MSC-CM in the published studies were purchased commercially from Lonza Inc. (US). This is a reagent purchase — buying an off-the-shelf research/clinical-grade cell product as manufacturing input — and is not evidence Lonza holds any claim on the finished secretome technology, the clinical application, or any IP arising from it. Flagged here so the distinction is visible rather than assumed either way, consistent with this agent's Gate 3 rule.

## What a US development path plausibly costs

Not scoped in detail this run — flagged as the next step, not estimated here without better information. The rough shape of the problem, for context:

- **Regulatory pathway is likely a full biologic (PHS Act §351 / BLA)** in the US, by the same reasoning FDA has applied to other secreted/cell-derived products (see the parallel discussion in [`jurisdiction-map.md`](jurisdiction-map.md) regarding exosomes) — there is no indication a 505(b)(2) or other shortcut applies to a conditioned-medium biologic. That means the standard cost and timeline profile of a de novo biologic program, not an abbreviated one.
- **Manufacturing and potency-assay development would need to start close to zero.** No CDMO-ready specification, no defined release assay, and no confirmed source cell line/banking strategy were found (this is the same category of gap [`cdmo-readiness.md`](../cdmo-readiness.md) maps for SGL-001 — worth reading that artifact alongside this one for the shape of the problem, not because the two products are related).
- **A randomized controlled trial against a real comparator (most likely autogenous bone graft or a marketed BMP-2 product) would be the first order of business** before any efficacy claim is defensible — neither existing study would satisfy a US regulator or a real diligence process on its own.

## Three questions a buyer would ask first

1. **Why has nothing happened to this in roughly ten years?** Is it neglect, or is there a known problem (manufacturing consistency, potency assay, an IP conflict, a failed internal attempt at translation) that hasn't made it into the public literature?
2. **What, exactly, is in the secretome, batch to batch?** MSC-CM is not a single defined molecule — it's a complex, biologically variable mixture. Without a release specification, "the same product" isn't yet a well-defined claim, and a regulator will ask this before a buyer needs to.
3. **Is there freedom to operate?** Patent status is unconfirmed in either direction. A buyer's first real cost here may be a clearance search, not a licensing negotiation.
