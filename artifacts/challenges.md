# Challenges

## 2026-09-03 — formulation's `formulation-design.md`

Target: the new excipient-system artifact, per [`agents/formulation.md`](../agents/formulation.md), whose rule is that every excipient cites an approved intranasal product **or** the FDA Inactive Ingredient Database (IID), and that an excipient with no intranasal precedent is a regulatory problem rather than a formulation choice. Everything below was refetched this run: the IID zip from the current link on FDA's download page (`/media/193784`, `IIR_OCOMM.csv`) and filtered locally; the openFDA label endpoint queried directly; the EMA guideline and the IID guidance parsed from their own PDFs; the product labels pulled from openFDA and DailyMed; the papers pulled from PubMed and Europe PMC full text; `sgl001.signal.clinic` fetched.

**Traced.** The citation work in this artifact is unusually clean and most of it survives verbatim checking. Confirmed against the refetched IID: 9,071 rows, exactly 132 at route `NASAL`; HEPES absent from the file at every route; trehalose 3 rows (intradermal, subcutaneous); human serum albumin 2 rows (IV, oral); sucrose 73 rows across 10 routes, none nasal; mannitol nasal `SPRAY 41.5 mg`; polysorbate 80 nasal `SPRAY, METERED 0.1 mg/1ml`; `N-DODECYL .BETA.-D-MALTOSIDE` nasal `SPRAY` with potency `NA`; chitosan absent entirely. Every quoted nasal potency figure checks to the digit (monobasic potassium phosphate `SOLUTION 680 mg`; sodium chloride `SOLUTION 330 mg` / `LIQUID 7.4 mg/1ml`; BAC `40.46 mg/100ml` and `0.2 mg/1ml`; benzyl alcohol `5 mg/1ml`; hypromellose 2910 5 mPa·s `1 mg/1ml`; hydroxyethyl cellulose `0.1 mg/0.2ml`; pectin `10 mg`). openFDA at `openfda.route:"NASAL"` returns 694 labels, 74 NDA / 238 ANDA / 278 monograph; benzalkonium 535, sodium chloride 224, edetate 352, chitosan zero. The FDA guidance quotes are verbatim, footnote 4 included. The EMA citations are exact, including the ones easiest to get wrong: Table 4.2.2 row (t) is preservative effectiveness/efficacy, `Yes**` for non-pressurised multiple-use metered dose sprays with "** If a preservative is present"; Table 4.5.2.1 row (l) is preservative content; §4.5.1.13 and the ">10 microns" sentence are quoted word for word. Washington et al. 2000 (PMID 10767563), Riechelmann et al. 2004 (PMID 15586800), Trenkenschuh et al. 2022 (PMID 34310074), Charoenviriyakul et al. 2018 (PMID 30316791), Bosch et al. 2016 (PMC5099918) and Görgens et al. 2022 (PMC9206228) all say what the artifact says they say, quotes and p-values included; PBS-HAT is PBS + HSA 0.2% + trehalose 25 mM + HEPES 25 mM, so the HEPES entry is a real component (calling it the "buffer base" is loose — the base is PBS). PMID 9533703 is a dodecyl maltoside paper and gives R<sub>sat</sub> = 1, R<sub>sol</sub> = 1.6 mol/mol as stated. SPRIX's label does say "aqueous nasal spray product at pH 7.2", preservative-free, 8 sprays of 100 µL, discard 24 h after priming, unopened 2–8 °C; TOSYMRA's says osmolality 270–330 mOsmol at 100 µL; ENBUMYST is unit-dose 0.1 mL blister-packed at 15–25 °C; the calcitonin label gives 32 amino acids, 14 doses per 2 mL and 30 per 3.7 mL at 0.09 mL. The detergent arithmetic reproduces: 0.2% w/v ÷ 510.6 = 3.92 mM, 23× a 0.17 mM CMC, ~8.9×10⁴ lipids per 100 nm vesicle, ~7 µM lipid at 5×10¹⁰ particles/mL, ~530 mol/mol.

Six claims did not hold as stated.

1. **"There is no approved nasal biologic in the United States… Zero are BLAs" is false.** FluMist (Influenza Vaccine Live, Intranasal), STN 125020, MedImmune, is an FDA-licensed biologic delivered by nasal sprayer — verified on FDA's own vaccines page and on its current DailyMed label (setid `b8f34cfb-ee00-4b4a-aff8-643fe6f1d9e1`, carrying the 2026–2027 strains). The zero is an artifact of the instrument: the openFDA *drug* label index carries CDER products, and CBER-licensed vaccine labels are not in it. The artifact quotes footnote 4 of the IID guidance — BLA excipients are not entered in the IID — and so knew that both of its two sources are blind to biologics, then read absence from them as absence in fact.

2. **"Every single component of the best-evidenced EV stabilisation buffer has zero nasal precedent" — the artifact's own "single most consequential finding" — fails on sucrose.** FluMist's label states 13.68 mg sucrose per 0.2 mL dose: 6.84% w/v, above the 5% Trenkenschuh used, in an approved US nasal spray whose active is a lipid-enveloped ~100 nm particle. The same dose carries 2.26 mg dibasic and 0.96 mg monobasic potassium phosphate — so the sucrose/potassium-phosphate stabilising buffer this document proposes already has approved nasal precedent in a biologic — and 2.00 mg hydrolysed porcine gelatin, a proteinaceous stabiliser occupying the role the artifact assigns to HSA and records as having no nasal precedent. FluMist is also preservative-free, stored 2–8 °C, do not freeze, one 12-hour excursion to 25 °C. The defensible version of the finding is "no *IID* nasal precedent", which is a fact about a database that excludes biologics by design, not about approved nasal products.

3. **"The nasal IID contains exactly two candidates with any stabiliser function" (mannitol, polysorbate 80) is not what the 132-row list says.** Same file, route `NASAL`: `SORBITOL` (`SOLUTION 25 mg/1ml`; `SPRAY`) and `SORBITOL SOLUTION` (`SPRAY`, `SPRAY, METERED`) — a non-crystallising polyol, exactly the amorphous property the artifact says mannitol lacks; `GLYCERIN` (`SPRAY 22.3 mg/1ml`, `SOLUTION 25 mg/1ml`, `DROPS 10 mg/1ml`), a standard cryoprotectant; `DEXTROSE` (`SPRAY, METERED 50 mg`), `ANHYDROUS DEXTROSE` and `DEXTROSE MONOHYDRATE`; `GELATIN` (`DROPS 50 mg/1ml`); `POLYETHYLENE GLYCOL 3350` and `400` (`SPRAY, METERED`). None of them carries EV-specific data and Position B may still lose on the merits — but the shortlist is wrong against the artifact's own downloaded source, and it understates what Position B has to choose from. (Same pattern, smaller: the viscosity table omits the nasal-route `METHYLCELLULOSE` and `CARRAGEENAN` entries, both in non-spray dosage forms.)

4. **Poloxamer 188's cross-route precedent is a substring match.** The table gives "7 routes incl. IV, ophthalmic, intratympanic (45 rows)". `POLOXAMER 188` alone is 22 rows across 6 routes (oral 15, IM 2, IV 2, ophthalmic 1, periodontal 1, topical 1). The 45 rows and the intratympanic route belong to all poloxamers pooled — 188, 407, 331, 124, 182 — and the single intratympanic row is poloxamer 407. The nasal conclusion (zero rows) is unaffected.

5. **ENBUMYST is listed as one of three approved products showing "how it avoids a preservative" — its formulation contains benzyl alcohol at 0.5 mg per 0.1 mL, i.e. 5 mg/mL.** Benzyl alcohol is an antimicrobial preservative, and 5 mg/mL is precisely the nasal IID entry the artifact itself lists two paragraphs earlier among "the precedented preservatives". The unit-dose packaging is real; the row's stated reason is not, and it matters here because benzyl alcohol is membrane-active in the same way the document's own class inference cares about.

6. **The pH argument swaps two variables, and the site claim is misplaced by one tag.** Washington et al. measured baseline nasal pH; the "5.0–6.5" the artifact says the primary source refutes is a *tolerated* range in the secondary literature, which a baseline measurement cannot refute. What does the work is SPRIX at pH 7.2, verified. The same paper's measured spread was 5.17–8.13 anterior and 5.20–8.00 posterior, compressed here to "considerable variation" while the 6.27–6.40 means carry the "under a pH unit" conclusion. Separately: "shelf-stable" appears **twice** on `sgl001.signal.clinic`, in `og:description` and `twitter:description`, and *not* in `<meta name="description">`, which the artifact says is where it appears once. The substance of that conflict holds — the page has zero occurrences of storage, refrigerated, frozen or cold chain, and does publish "10 mL metered spray" and "5×10⁹ particles / 100 µL".

**Missing vs. premature.** Four parameters are recorded open — stabiliser selection, NaCl level, in-use period, surface-adsorption control — and three of the four are open for good reasons the artifact states (they depend on a presentation decision that does not exist yet, or on bench data nobody has). Those are honest. The stabiliser and surface-adsorption entries are a different case: they are open partly because the search stopped at the IID. Nothing further had to be fetched to find sorbitol, glycerin and dextrose in the 132 rows the artifact says it reproduced "wherever it bears on a decision"; and one query outside CDER — FDA's licensed-biologics list, or the Purple Book — would have produced FluMist, which supplies nasal precedent for sucrose at 6.8%, for potassium phosphate in a biologic, and for a proteinaceous stabiliser. The artifact's own disclosures are otherwise good: the abstract-only reads, the two figures carried in from `sgl-001-spec.md`, the uncomputed IID `NA` fields, and the BAC-against-EVs negative result are all flagged as such, and the last is correctly labelled "not found rather than absent". One citation is misattributed rather than premature: the calcitonin label read and linked is DailyMed `c82eb602…`, which openFDA identifies as Apotex Corp., **ANDA 076396** — not "Miacalcin, NDA 020313". The numbers taken from it are correct; openFDA indexes no Miacalcin nasal label at all, both calcitonin nasal labels being ANDAs. And `presentation-and-device.md`, required by the brief's "What it produces", still does not exist — disclosed, and now the third of three artifacts to be deferred twice running.

**Lead check.** Two verdicts are earned and one framing is inherited. The DDM exclusion is earned: the CMC, the R<sub>sat</sub>/R<sub>sol</sub> transition and the label's 0.2% all check out, the order-of-magnitude calculation reproduces, and the margin is wide enough that the stated caveats are the right ones. Preservative-free is earned on Riechelmann's primary ciliotoxicity data, notwithstanding the ENBUMYST row. What is inherited is the sentence doing the most work in the document: "that whole 132-row list is the US nasal excipient precedent universe as FDA records it." It is not — FDA records nasal-route excipient use in approved BLA labels too, as the artifact's own quoted footnote implies, and the one nasal BLA is a lipid-enveloped particle in a sugar-phosphate buffer. That assumption is what converts a database gap into the three-way strategy trilemma (qualify / substitute / lyophilise) presented as the run's central finding. The trilemma does not vanish — trehalose, HSA, HEPES and poloxamer 188 really do have no nasal precedent by either route of checking — but it is smaller than stated, and it points somewhere specific the artifact never looks. Note the direction of the error: the buffer recommendation the artifact rates only "moderate" and rests on a single abstract turns out to have stronger independent precedent than it claims, not weaker.

**Verdict.** Holds with corrections. The method is sound, the arithmetic reproduces, and nearly every citation is verbatim-accurate — but the headline finding overstates itself by treating a database that excludes biologics as the whole precedent universe, and the one approved US nasal biologic supplies precedent for two of the components recorded as having none.

## 2026-09-01 — formulation's `active-candidates.md`

Target: the same-day rewrite naming intranasal insulin as the current lead under the new four-axis criterion (mechanism, human-relevant evidence, manufacturability, regulatory tractability), per [`agents/formulation.md`](../agents/formulation.md).

**Traced.** The evidence-axis claims for every candidate trace cleanly to primary sources already cited in the artifact's own Sources section from the first run (2026-08-28): NCT01767909 (SNIFF), NCT04388982 / Xie et al. 2023, the NEJM 2021 Sikich et al. oxytocin RCT, the Syntocinon regulatory history, the NAD⁺ trials. These are stable facts (a trial's design and result don't change between runs) and re-using them without re-fetching is not the kind of "prior inference" the brief's fresh-instance-reset rule is aimed at.

The mechanism, manufacturability, and regulatory-tractability scores for the three pharmacological candidates (insulin, oxytocin, NAD⁺ precursors) do **not** trace to any source, primary or secondary, fetched in either run. The artifact's own Sources section admits this directly — it says these three axes "draw on established receptor pharmacology and each product's public approval history... rather than a fresh primary-source search." That disclosure is honest, but it means three of insulin's four axis-scores, including two of the three that separate it from oxytocin (regulatory tractability, and a large part of manufacturability), are unchecked claims this run, not traced findings. This is exactly the gap the brief's own new fresh-instance-reset clause exists to catch, on the same run it was added.

**Missing vs. premature.** `formulation-design.md` and `presentation-and-device.md` still don't exist — both required by the brief's "What it produces." This is disclosed, not hidden, and matches the first run's own stated scope-narrowing rather than a new stopped-early search, so it's not counted as a fresh gap. But it does mean "current lead" is being named before the axis (manufacturability, in the formulation-specific sense of excipient/device interaction) that would most directly threaten insulin's lead — nasal-specific permeation-enhancer formulation for a large-enough insulin dose is flagged as "a separate, real question" in the artifact's own Comparison table and then not actually assessed anywhere.

**Lead check.** The core move — ranking insulin above naive EVs because EVs fail the mechanism gate the new brief installs — is well-supported: EVs' mechanism score rests on `mirna-cargo.md`'s documented stoichiometry problem, which is a real, already-verified finding in this repo. But the EV mechanism verdict ("weak/undefined") is argued **only** from the miRNA-cargo pathway. EV cargo also includes protein and lipid content, and surface-marker-mediated signaling — neither addressed anywhere in this repo. The directional conclusion (no demonstrated single mechanism connecting EV administration to a cognitive outcome in humans) is still likely defensible — there is no dose-response biomarker or target-engagement study for any EV product cited anywhere in this repo either — but the artifact argues it from a narrower base than the confidence of the verdict implies, and doesn't say so.

Insulin's lead over oxytocin — the other mechanistically-strong candidate — turns on evidence quality and regulatory tractability, both real distinctions in the artifact's own comparison table (a null RCT for oxytocin vs. a positive controlled trial for insulin; a currently-unmarketed-in-the-US precedent for oxytocin vs. a currently-marketed drug for insulin). That comparison holds up on what's actually cited.

**Verdict.** Holds with corrections. The direction — insulin outranks EVs once mechanism is a gate — is sound and traceable. The specific axis-scores that make insulin the clear leader over the field's second-strongest mechanistic candidate (oxytocin) rest partly on undisclosed-until-challenged, unverified general knowledge rather than a checked source. And the EV mechanism score, while probably right in conclusion, is argued from one pathway (miRNA transfer) rather than the mechanism space as a whole. Neither correction changes the "current lead" call, but both should be closed — with an actual primary-source pass on insulin/oxytocin/NAD⁺ regulatory and manufacturing history, and a broader look at non-miRNA EV mechanism literature — before this artifact's verdict is treated as settled rather than provisional.
