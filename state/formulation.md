# Formulation agent — state

## 2026-09-03 — fourth run: `formulation-design.md` created, excipient system

**Scope this run:** the brief's artifact 2, `artifacts/formulation-design.md`, which had been open since the first run. Created from scratch. Deliberately did not attempt `presentation-and-device.md` — the device parameters it needs (actuation volume tolerance, dead volume, priming, plume geometry) were not researched this run, and an artifact whose every row said "open" would be filler. Three findings that feed it are recorded inside `formulation-design.md` so the next run starts from them.

**Method changed this run, and it mattered.** Prior runs worked from the literature. This run went to the regulatory data directly: downloaded the **FDA Inactive Ingredient Database, July 2026 release**, in full (9,071 rows) and filtered locally to the **132 route-`NASAL`** entries; queried the **openFDA drug label index** filtered to `openfda.route:"NASAL"` (694 labels) for named products and levels; and read five approved labels as labels (TOSYMRA NDA 210884, calcitonin-salmon NDA 020313, SPRIX NDA 022382, ENBUMYST NDA 219500, BAQSIMI NDA 210134). The EMA nasal guideline PDF was downloaded and parsed rather than cited from memory. That whole 132-row nasal list is small enough to read end to end, which is why several of this run's findings are exhaustive claims rather than search results.

**The finding that dominates the artifact: the EV stabilisation toolkit has zero nasal precedent.** Checked against the IID by route — trehalose (intradermal, subcutaneous only), human serum albumin (IV, oral only), sucrose (10 routes, no nasal), poloxamer 188 (7 routes, no nasal), HEPES (**absent from the IID entirely, any route**). Every component of PBS-HAT (Görgens et al. 2022) and of Trenkenschuh's lyophilisate has no nasal-route prior use. Under the brief's own rule that an excipient with no intranasal precedent is a regulatory problem rather than a formulation choice, the best-evidenced way to keep EVs intact is not currently a formulation option without a qualification package. The only nasal-precedented substitutes with any stabiliser function are mannitol (IID nasal SPRAY, 41.5 mg — a crystallising bulking agent, weaker for a membrane particle, no EV data found) and polysorbate 80 (0.1 mg/mL metered spray — but a surfactant above its own CMC).

**Second finding: the one approved nasal permeation enhancer would dissolve the active.** The nasal IID contains exactly one permeation enhancer, n-dodecyl β-D-maltoside (TOSYMRA, 0.2% w/v). DDM's CMC is 0.17 mM; 0.2% is 3.92 mM, ~23× CMC, and above CMC it solubilises bilayers (Rsat ≈ 1, Rsol ≈ 1.6 mol/mol, *Biophys J* 1998). Against the site's own 5×10¹⁰ particles/mL target that is roughly 500 mol detergent per mol lipid. Ruled out. **This leaves the permeation problem unsolved, not solved** — with no enhancer, the delivered olfactory fraction is the entire lever, which makes `sgl-001-spec.md`'s open deposition question more load-bearing, not less.

**Third finding, and a correction to what the secondary literature says.** Nasal formulation reviews routinely give tolerated nasal pH as 5.0–6.5. The primary in-vivo measurement does not: Washington et al. 2000 (*Int J Pharm* 198:139–146, n=12, electrodes in the nasal cavity) found anterior pH **6.40** and posterior **6.27**. EVs prefer neutral (Trenkenschuh et al. 2022). So the mucosa/active pH conflict the brief asks about is **much smaller than expected** — under a pH unit — and is already bridged by an approved product, SPRIX at pH 7.2. Proposed 10 mM potassium phosphate at pH 7.0–7.2; potassium phosphate has nasal precedent (IID, TOSYMRA, SPRIX) *and* is independently the buffer EV freeze-thaw work favours over sodium phosphate and PBS. That convergence was not expected.

**Two structural facts recorded before any excipient was picked.** (1) **There is no approved nasal biologic in the US** — of the nasal labels with an application number, 74 NDA, 238 ANDA, 278 OTC monograph, **zero BLA**. (2) FDA's IID guidance states outright that "excipients used in approved Biologics License Applications (BLAs) are not entered into the IID." So the nasal precedent universe is entirely small-molecule and peptide NDA/ANDA. It is the right database to check and it is still a neighbouring field, not the same one. Both cap how strong any precedent claim in the artifact can be.

**New conflict recorded, and flagged to spec rather than edited into it.** `sgl001.signal.clinic` (fetched 2026-09-03) describes the product in its meta description as "a shelf-stable intranasal exosome biologic" while publishing no storage condition, no shelf life, and no formulation. `sgl-001-spec.md`'s 2026-09-01 check recorded the *absence* of a storage condition but did not catch the affirmative stability claim sitting alongside it. Recorded in `formulation-design.md`'s Conflicts section; the spec agent owns the product-parameter record, so it is flagged there, not edited into that artifact.

**Useful side finding for the doses-per-container arithmetic.** The calcitonin-salmon label states a 2 mL fill yields "at least 14 doses" of 0.09 mL (~63% of fill delivered) and a 3.7 mL fill yields 30 doses (~73%). That is independent, label-sourced primary support for `sgl-001-spec.md`'s reasoning about the site's withdrawn "100 doses" figure — a 10 mL fill at 100 µL implies roughly 63–73 deliverable doses, not 100.

**Preservative question resolved in direction, not in number.** Preservative-free, on two grounds: BAC is ciliotoxic at nasal use concentrations (Riechelmann et al. 2004, p < 0.0001, human epithelia from 15 donors, plus irritation/burning/hypersecretion in vivo at 0.05%), and it is a cationic surfactant in continuous contact with a lipid-vesicle active. The three approved preservative-free routes are unit-dose (TOSYMRA, ENBUMYST) or a very short in-use limit (SPRIX: multi-dose, 100 µL metered, **discard within 24 hours of priming**). SPRIX is the closest structural analogue to what SGL-001 publishes and it buys preservative-free with 8 doses and one day. The in-use period for this product stays **open** — it cannot be set without stability data.

**Recorded as open rather than guessed:** the stabiliser selection (Position A, qualify trehalose, vs Position B, stay inside the precedent set — stated preference for A, no decision taken), the NaCl level (depends on the stabiliser's osmolality contribution), the in-use period, and the surface-adsorption control. No viscosity or mucoadhesive agent in the initial design — a deliberate deferral against the plume-quality/olfactory-deposition trade-off, not an omission, and reversible.

**`active-candidates.md` touched only where it cross-referenced this artifact.** Its "Not assessed this run" section said excipient constraints belonged to a document that didn't exist; it now records what does and doesn't get settled, and notes the two findings above make the EV rows' manufacturability and regulatory scores harder rather than easier. **No score in the four-axis table was changed, and none should be read as changed by this run.** The equivalent excipient work has not been done for the peptide, secretome, or NAD⁺ rows, so that column is filled for one candidate, not for the comparison.

**Sourcing honesty for this run:** Trenkenschuh et al. 2022 and Charoenviriyakul et al. 2018 were read as **abstracts only**, via Europe PMC, not full text — and the buffer recommendation rests substantially on the Trenkenschuh abstract, which is a real weakness a challenge pass should press on. The Ahmadian et al. 2024 freeze-thaw figures and the Xi et al. 2016 deposition fraction were carried in from `sgl-001-spec.md` and not re-fetched from their own publications, though both are load-bearing here. The ~500 mol/mol DDM:lipid ratio is this run's own arithmetic, not a cited value; the inputs are stated in the artifact so it can be checked or discarded. No study of any nasal preservative against EV preparations was found — a negative search result, not evidence of absence.

**Open for a future run:**

- `presentation-and-device.md`. The liquid/lyophilised/frozen comparison now has real inputs: TOSYMRA and ENBUMYST as room-temperature unit-dose precedents, SPRIX as the multi-dose preservative-free precedent, and Trenkenschuh's lyophilisate holding size and concentration 6 months at 40 °C — the only published route to anything resembling "shelf-stable" for this active, and it is a cake, not a spray.
- Read Trenkenschuh et al. 2022 in full before the buffer recommendation is relied on further.
- Excipient supplier, compendial grade, and DMF availability were not checked at all. Neither were extractables/leachables (EMA §4.5.1.11).
- Several IID nasal entries relied on carry no maximum potency (the MCC/CMC-Na co-processed entry, hypromellose 4000 mPa·s, DDM). Precedent of *use* exists; a precedented *level* does not, and those levels would need independent justification.

**Result: the current lead is intranasal insulin, not extracellular vesicles.** Applied honestly, the four-axis criterion favours insulin on all four axes — defined mechanism, the deepest controlled human cognitive dataset in the comparison (SNIFF), approved-biologic manufacturability, and an easier regulatory starting point (off-label device repurposing) than any cell- or vesicle-derived candidate. ## 2026-08-28 — first run

**Scope this run:** Per operator instruction — `artifacts/active-candidates.md` only. Explicitly did not attempt `artifacts/formulation-design.md` (excipient system) or `artifacts/presentation-and-device.md` (presentation/device) this run. Within the active-candidates comparison itself, scope was further narrowed to three of the brief's eight columns — human intranasal precedent, regulatory class, and molecular size/stability profile — leaving excipient/pH constraints, cost and supply, combinability, and verdict unfilled rather than guessed.

**What changed:** `artifacts/active-candidates.md` created from scratch. Eight rows: naive/unmodified EVs, engineered/loaded EVs, naive whole-cell (MSC/NSC) intranasal delivery, MSC secretome/conditioned medium, intranasal insulin, intranasal oxytocin, NAD⁺ precursors, and a non-candidate reference row (calcitonin-salmon/Miacalcin) included purely as the benchmark for what an approved, marketed nasal protein's regulatory and stability profile actually looks like.

**What the search found, by candidate:**

- **Naive EVs:** three human intranasal trials exist, not one — this is new relative to what `sgl-001-spec.md` currently reflects. Xie et al. 2023/NCT04388982 (Alzheimer's, n=9, open-label) was already known. NCT06598202 (ALS, n=20, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled, still recruiting) and NCT07105371 (Prodromos 2025, n=18 completed, several motor-neuron/movement disorders) are new to this repo. Only the Xie trial targets a cognitive/dementia endpoint; the other two are motor-neuron indications — a different mechanistic claim than Signal's own cognitive framing, worth being precise about rather than counting all three as equally supportive of a cognitive claim.
- **Engineered/loaded EVs:** zero human intranasal precedent found anywhere. Every engineered-cargo intranasal EV study located is a rodent or in vitro model. If this direction is ever pursued, it starts from zero human data, not as an increment on naive EVs.
- **Naive whole-cell intranasal delivery (not exosomes):** more aggregate human trial activity than EVs — NEST (NCT02795052, n≈500 planned, still recruiting after 9+ years, no results), Boika et al. 2020 (Parkinson's, Phase II/III, n=12, positive signal), and Lv et al. 2023 (NCT03005249, cerebral palsy, Phase I/II, n=25, completed, positive imaging and functional outcome, rated the highest-quality trial in the field by a 2026 systematic review). But live-cell delivery has a stability profile — viability-dependent, almost certainly precluding lyophilization or long room-temperature storage — that may be incompatible with a shelf-stable multi-dose consumer product regardless of how good the human data gets.
- **MSC secretome/conditioned medium:** one human intranasal trial found, and it's not neurological — NCT02192736, asthma, Phase I/II, n=20, weekly ×4 weeks, safe with a positive pulmonary/QoL signal. For a cognitive claim specifically, only preclinical (rodent) intranasal secretome data exists. Separately noted, without importing the BD framing: Nagoya University's published MSC-conditioned-medium work (already documented in the private `japan-asset-longlist.md`) is local-injection bone regeneration, not intranasal and not neuro — cited here for the underlying science only, which is a legitimate public reference; the licensing context around that specific asset stays in the private file where it belongs.
- **Intranasal insulin:** the deepest controlled human cognitive dataset of any candidate compared — SNIFF (NCT01767909), Phase II, ~240 participants, 12 months, MCI/Alzheimer's, randomized placebo-controlled, with favorable CSF and white-matter-hyperintensity findings, though a 2026 pooled review notes real heterogeneity by sex, APOE genotype, and delivery device. Regulatory and stability position is categorically easier than every cell- or vesicle-derived candidate, since it's a repurposing of an already-approved biologic — but it is not itself a regenerative or cell-derived active, which matters for whether it even fits what Signal is otherwise positioned as.
- **Intranasal oxytocin:** real mixed evidence, not a clean positive — the NEJM 2021 pediatric autism trial found no significant social/cognitive benefit over 24 weeks despite smaller trials showing dose-dependent effects. Included deliberately as the candidate with the clearest example of a nasal peptide *failing* to show a cognitive effect in a well-run trial, not just as a positive comparator. Was FDA-approved (Syntocinon, discontinued 1997 for commercial reasons, still marketed in Europe) — a real precedent for the regulatory pathway even without a current US-marketed product.
- **NAD⁺ precursors:** no intranasal human trial found at all — every human cognitive data point located is oral, and even orally the cognitive evidence is weak/mixed (a long-COVID RCT raised NAD⁺ levels without improving cognition). Currently a US dietary supplement, not a drug — a disease/cognitive claim would move it into drug regulation entirely, changing its regulatory profile from the ground up.
- **Reference row (calcitonin-salmon/Miacalcin):** not a candidate, included only to establish what an actually-approved, actually-marketed nasal protein spray's stability profile looks like (2–8°C unopened, 30–35 days room-temperature in-use once opened) — the practical bar every biologic candidate above is implicitly being measured against.

**Open questions carried forward:**

- `sgl-001-spec.md` should be updated to reflect that three human intranasal EV trials exist, not one, and specifically that NCT06598202 is the only *controlled* one found — this belongs to spec now, per the new division of labor, not to this agent to edit directly.
- The excipient/pH-constraint, cost-and-supply, and combinability columns are unfilled by design this run. A verdict (lead/secondary/excluded) should not be assigned before at least the excipient-constraint column exists, since a candidate's molecular fragility interacts directly with what excipient system could stabilize it.
- Every "systematic review" citation in this run's source list (the 2026 *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience* review in particular) was used as a secondary source and not independently checked against each underlying primary registry entry. If any of the trials it surfaced (NEST, Boika et al., Lv et al., NCT02192736, NCT06598202, NCT07105371) becomes a serious candidate in a future run, go to the primary registry/publication directly rather than continuing to rely on the review's summary.
- Whole-cell intranasal delivery was not in the brief's minimum candidate list but turned up as a real, well-precedented alternative to EVs during this search — worth keeping as a permanent row rather than treating it as a one-off addition.
